Module 2 · Applications: a classifier on CESM2 LENS

Does the autumn ENSO state predict a wet Front Range winter? Overview¶

You build a classifier that reads one number — the autumn Niño 3.4 state of a CESM2 LENS member — and says whether that member’s coming Front Range winter will be wet, on the 100 members of the NSF NCAR-distributed large ensemble. At the end you decide, in writing, whether the model that survived the refinement trials — or the simplest rule it had to beat — deserves to be used, justified as Unidata’s closing instruction asks: “using data and a robust evaluation”.

Objectives:

  1. O2 — the steps of a supervised analysis and the failure each step guards against: framing → data → split → train → validate → refine → test → justify.

  2. O3 — build and validate a classification model on a climate-model ensemble, reporting precision, recall and a confusion matrix, and justify the choice honestly, including when the simplest wins.

The spine is NSF Unidata’s (Apache-2.0), with one step changed on purpose: Unidata splits randomly; here the split is by ensemble member, and 2c says why.

Exercises 1–11. Each exercise box here is a one-line pointer to its Handbook entry (handbook.md); the Handbook prompt, with its evidence list, is the prompt of record and what the Rubric (rubric.md) scores, first by you, then by the trainer.

Does a simpler solution exist? Three do — the table below: two bars and the physical prior, which 3a scores and Entry 6 records. None is Module 1’s closed-form case: no known relation gives winter precipitation from autumn sea-surface temperature exactly. The relation is statistical and, by the published evidence, weak — so a model is measured against the three rules rather than assumed to beat them, and a rule that sits below the base-rate bar, however physical, is not the simplest solution; the base rate itself is.

Simple solutionWhat it predictsThe bar it sets, or the prior it is (3a’s rung 0; Entry 6 scores it)

Can machine learning requirements be met? On the data side, yes: the catalogue holds monthly PRECC, PRECL and TS for every historical member in both forcing variants — 100 members × 115 winters with a measurable target and a measurable input. On the physics side the honest prior is a small effect: Stagge et al. find the ENSO winter-precipitation signal over the Intermountain West is a north–south dipole, “the northern pole … north of 40° N” and “the southern pole … south of 35° N”, with the Front Range in the transition zone between them, and Rodgers et al. 2021 report no Front Range value for CESM2 LENS. The signal is something this notebook measures, not assumes — and if it is weak, the simplest rule winning is the honest answer, not a failure.

Which scientific question should be answered? “Given the autumn Niño 3.4 state in a CESM2 LENS member, will that member’s coming Front Range winter be wet — above the one pooled threshold that marks the upper third of the training members’ detrended winters?” Written as a decision: the person who acts on it is the forecaster drafting the autumn seasonal outlook for the Front Range — the deck NWS Boulder and NWS Pueblo issue each September — who must lean the winter outlook wet, dry, or leave it at “equal chances”. A “wet” call right more often than the base rate without missing most wet winters would move that lean; a model that cannot beat the sign rule leaves it where the office already puts it.

Deriving the member-winter table (one-time; cached)¶

Each Zarr chunk holds a whole global field, so deriving two box means for 100 members reads roughly 160 GB (the chunking line above computes it from the store: chunks × members × variables). The derivation runs once, writes data/member_winters.csv, and every later cell reads the cache; REBUILD = True re-derives it with the parameters above — the record of how the table was made is the code itself. 2b Explore the target and the inputs¶

Three things are fit here, in an order that matters. First the test members are named — TEST_FRACTION of each half’s members, drawn by member from a RandomState the cell creates from RNG_SEED right before the draw (re-running the cell redraws the same members) — and nothing below is fit on them. Then the forced response is removed. Every member follows the same forcing path by calendar year, so the raw SON Niño 3.4 box mean and the winter precipitation both carry a forced trend (printed below as their correlation with year; Rodgers et al. 2021 §3.2). A smooth fit to the training-member ensemble mean is subtracted from every member alike, leaving the member’s own departure from the shared path — the ENSO state, not the decade. Then the target is defined against one threshold: wet = 1 when the detrended precipitation sits above the WET_QUANTILE of the pooled training members. Curve and threshold are transforms fit on training members and applied to test members — as a StandardScaler inside a Pipeline is (scikit-learn’s Common pitfalls and recommended practices, §12.2.1) — written as fit_transforms and apply_transforms so that every validation fold refits them. Then the removal is checked, with a line that can fail: the training members’ mean anomaly per decade, in standard errors, against a stated tolerance.

Read the base rate first: with WET_QUANTILE = 2/3 it is about one third, so accuracy of 0.67 is what “always dry” scores — why this module reports precision and recall against the base rate (scikit-learn’s Classification metrics guide).

def fit_transforms(rows):
    """Fit on `rows` ONLY (all training members here; a fold's training members in 2c/3c):
    the forced curve of each series — a degree-FORCED_DEG polynomial in year fitted to the ensemble mean of `rows` — and
    the wet threshold — the WET_QUANTILE of the pooled detrended precipitation of `rows`."""
    curves = {}
    for anom, raw in ANOMALIES.items():
        ens_mean = rows.groupby("winter")[raw].mean().reindex(years)
        curves[anom] = pd.Series(np.polyval(np.polyfit(years, ens_mean.values, FORCED_DEG), years), index=years)
    prect_anom = pd.Series(rows["prect_winter_mm_day"].values - curves["prect_anom"].reindex(rows["winter"]).values)
    return dict(curves=curves, wet_threshold=float(prect_anom.quantile(WET_QUANTILE)))

def apply_transforms(rows, tf):
    """Subtract the fitted curves and label `wet` against the fitted threshold, on any rows (held-out ones included). Fits nothing."""
    out = rows.copy()
    for anom, raw in ANOMALIES.items():
        out[anom] = out[raw].values - tf["curves"][anom].reindex(out["winter"]).values
    out["wet"] = (out["prect_anom"] > tf["wet_threshold"]).astype(int)
    return out

2c Split by ensemble member¶

This is the step the course changes from Unidata’s notebooks (they split “randomly”), and the one O2’s split failure names: training and scoring sets that share ensemble members, so the “unseen” score is inflated. scikit-learn’s guidance on grouped cross-validation (BSD-3-Clause): “all the samples in the validation fold come from groups that are not represented at all in the paired training fold.”

Here the group is the member, for three reasons the code below makes measurable:

  1. The question is about members never seen. Part 1 asks what the autumn state says about a member’s coming winter, so the score has to be on a realisation the model has not met; a member-blind split scores new years of members it already knows.

  2. The normalisation constants. The forced curve and the wet threshold are fit on training members only (2b) and refit inside every validation fold (score_splits calls fit_transforms per fold). Fit before the split, the held-out winters would help fit the curve and the threshold that label them — the same leak as a StandardScaler fit on the whole table; Exercise 5 asks what that score would measure.

  3. Dependence within and between members is measured, not assumed. Rodgers et al. 2021 §2.2 is why WINDOW starts late — initial-condition memory fades over decades — but Rodgers give no figure for how much is left after fifty years, so the cell prints the mean correlation of the detrended series between micro-siblings (the same start year, as the CESM2 LE project page defines them) and between unrelated training members, with a line that fails if siblings share more. Siblings are not grouped in the split; that line is what licenses it, and Entry 5 asks you to paste it. The same cell prints the lag-1 autocorrelation inside members, and the contrast cell scores one model three ways on the same members (the table below): with one input that carries no member identity, whether either leak bites here is a number, not a claim.

# The measured contrast (Exercise 5). One function scores every validation run in this notebook: score_splits.
def score_splits(model, rows, splits, refit_transforms=True):
    """Fit on each split's training rows, score its held-out rows. With refit_transforms=True (this notebook's pipeline) the forced
    curves and the wet threshold are refit on the split's training rows only (fit_transforms, 2b) and applied to both sides, so a
    held-out row is centred and labelled by transforms it did not help fit. With False the columns already in `rows` are used: the
    transforms fit once on ALL training members, before the folds — so a held-out fold member helped fit them; the test members are still outside. Returns per-fold precision, recall, F1, accuracy and AP for class 1
    (wet), plus the summed confusion matrix (rows = true, columns = predicted)."""
    out = dict(precision=[], recall=[], f1=[], accuracy=[], ap=[]); cms = []
    for a, b in splits:
        fit_rows, held_rows = rows.iloc[a], rows.iloc[b]
        if refit_transforms:
            tf = fit_transforms(fit_rows)
            fit_rows, held_rows = apply_transforms(fit_rows, tf), apply_transforms(held_rows, tf)
        Xa, ya, Xb, yb = fit_rows[FEATURES].values, fit_rows["wet"].values, held_rows[FEATURES].values, held_rows["wet"].values
        model.fit(Xa, ya); yhat = model.predict(Xb)
        score = model.predict_proba(Xb)[:, 1] if hasattr(model, "predict_proba") else model.decision_function(Xb)
        out["precision"].append(precision_score(yb, yhat, zero_division=0)); out["recall"].append(recall_score(yb, yhat, zero_division=0))
        out["f1"].append(f1_score(yb, yhat, zero_division=0)); out["accuracy"].append(accuracy_score(yb, yhat))
        out["ap"].append(average_precision_score(yb, score)); cms.append(confusion_matrix(yb, yhat, labels=[0, 1]))
    return {k: np.array(v) for k, v in out.items()}, np.sum(cms, axis=0)

3d Validate — confusion matrix, precision, recall¶

Validation guards against O2’s validate failure: tuning with no held-out score to show whether the model is memorising. The confusion matrix follows scikit-learn’s convention — rows = true class, columns = predicted (scikit-learn’s Classification metrics guide); precision reads down the “predicted wet” column, recall along the “actual wet” row. Both are for the wet class, and both are read against the base rate.

The table carries two estimators of the same thing, labelled. precision_fold / recall_fold are the mean (± SD) of the per-fold values, with zero_division=0, so a fold in which a model never predicts wet contributes 0. precision_pooled / recall_pooled are read off the summed confusion matrix — one division over all folds’ counts — which is what the matrices show and what Exercise 7 computes by hand; the two differ whenever folds differ in size or in how often the model predicted wet. Accuracy, F1 and average precision (AP) stand beside them because precision and recall alone can be gamed by predicting one class (always-wet has recall 1 at precision = base rate); a constant or random score has AP = base rate (the same guide).

def pooled(cm):

    tp, fp, fn = cm[1, 1], cm[0, 1], cm[1, 0]
    return dict(precision_pooled=tp / (tp + fp) if tp + fp else 0.0, recall_pooled=tp / (tp + fn) if tp + fn else 0.0)

3f Refinement trials — the paired test¶

Refinement guards against O2’s refine failure: stopping at the first configuration tried. Unidata’s instruction is to run trials “until … they no longer improve” — judged against the run-to-run spread. Scores from the same folds are not independent, so a plain t-test under-estimates the variance; the course uses the Nadeau–Bengio corrected paired t-test from scikit-learn’s own example, Statistical comparison of models using grid search (BSD-3-Clause). Which spread: the corrected standard error of the per-fold differences, which the _t column divides the gap by. The register also prints _sd, the plain per-fold standard deviation, for scale — on this design the corrected standard error is about half that per-fold SD, and about 3.7 times the naive standard error a plain paired t would use (that SD divided by the square root of the 50 paired scores): inflating the naive error is the point of the correction, and the factor is in corrected_std below — and _sd is not the test. A gap inside the corrected error is not a difference. The example warns that “if multiple models are statistically compared, a multiple comparisons correction is needed”; the register does not apply one — each printed p is per test, uncorrected — so it bounds the looks: six tested rows, two metrics each. Under every register the notebook prints the Bonferroni line, 0.05 ÷ the looks taken so far: a p under 0.05 but above that line is a promotion under the uncorrected rule only (design default 6).

What a trial is scored on. Recall alone rewards predicting wet more often (always-wet scores 1.0); precision alone rewards predicting it almost never; AP cannot see the threshold 3e teaches you to trial. So a candidate is promoted only if it improves one of the two numbers the decision uses measurably — corrected p < 0.05 with a positive gap — without giving the other back on average (mean gap ≥ 0); both tests sit in every register row. The six tested rows are spent as: the ladder’s three rungs and the example trial — four already spent when the notebook arrives — then up to two trials of your own.

What this rule cannot see. It compares a candidate with the incumbent only, and the first incumbent is the Niño-sign rule — the physical prior, not a rule that cleared a bar; your Entry 6 says whether its precision sits above or below the always-wet bar. If below, a candidate with no skill at all — precision merely at the base rate — can beat it on precision; the example trial can be exactly that, so read any promotion it earns as a promotion over the sign rule, not as skill. The 3g interval lines catch it, reading the tested model against the base rate of the same winters; Module 3’s 3f adds the guard this rule lacks (AP above the incumbent and the base rate).

# Copyright (c) 2007-2026 The scikit-learn developers. BSD-3-Clause; full text in LICENSES/scikit-learn-BSD-3-Clause.txt. From examples/model_selection/plot_grid_search_stats.py
def corrected_std(differences, n_train, n_test):
    """Corrects standard deviation using Nadeau and Bengio's approach."""
    kr = len(differences)
    corrected_var = np.var(differences, ddof=1) * (1 / kr + n_test / n_train)
    return np.sqrt(corrected_var)

def compute_corrected_ttest(differences, df, n_train, n_test):
    """Computes right-tailed paired t-test with corrected variance."""
    mean = np.mean(differences)
    std = corrected_std(differences, n_train, n_test)
    if std == 0:                      # course addition: identical scores in every fold — no spread, no difference (guards 0/0)
        return 0.0, 1.0
    t_stat = mean / std
    p_val = t_dist.sf(t_stat, df)       # course change: t_stat, not np.abs(t_stat) — a right tail as the docstring says, so a LOSS prints p → 1;
    return t_stat, p_val                #   the example's abs printed a loss's left-tail probability under a right-tailed label

METRICS = ["recall", "precision"]      # the two numbers the decision uses; both are tested in every trial

def trial(make_model, rows):
    """Repeat the member-grouped folds N_REPEATS_EFFECTIVE times with different fold draws; per-fold precision and recall (kr values each)."""
    scores = {m: [] for m in METRICS}
    for rep in range(N_REPEATS_EFFECTIVE):
        s, _ = run_folds(make_model(RNG_SEED + rep), rows, seed=RNG_SEED + rep)
        for m in METRICS:
            scores[m].extend(s[m])
    return {m: np.array(v) for m, v in scores.items()}

def compare(incumbent_scores, candidate_scores, n_rows, metric):
    """Corrected paired t-test on the per-fold differences of one metric (same fold draws for both models). Unrounded: verdict() reads
    these values; display_row() rounds them for the register, so a gap of +0.0004 or a p of 0.0496 is judged as itself, not as 0.000 or 0.050."""
    n_test = n_rows // N_FOLDS; n_train = n_rows - n_test
    diff = candidate_scores[metric] - incumbent_scores[metric]
    t_stat, p_val = compute_corrected_ttest(diff, df=len(diff) - 1, n_train=n_train, n_test=n_test)
    return {f"{metric}_cand": float(candidate_scores[metric].mean()), f"{metric}_inc": float(incumbent_scores[metric].mean()),
            f"{metric}_gap": float(diff.mean()), f"{metric}_sd": float(diff.std(ddof=1)),
            f"{metric}_t": float(t_stat), f"{metric}_p": float(p_val)}

def show_p(p):
    """A p-value to three significant figures — 0.0312, 0.00384, 1.3e-05 — so that any p can be placed against the Bonferroni line (4 dp):
    at 3 dp a p of 0.0038 and one of 0.0044 both printed 0.004, one on each side of the line at the cap (0.0042), and anything under 0.0005 printed 0.000."""
    return f"{p:.3g}"

def display_row(row):
    """The register's display precision — 3 dp; `_t` to 2 dp; `_p` to three significant figures (show_p). The verdict was taken on the
    unrounded values before this is called."""
    def shown(k, v):
        if not isinstance(v, float):
            return v
        if k.endswith("_t"):
            return round(v, 2)
        if k.endswith("_p"):
            return float(show_p(v))
        return round(v, 3)
    return {k: shown(k, v) for k, v in row.items()}

def verdict(row):
    """The 3f rule: promoted only if one metric improves measurably and the other does not fall on average. Read on the unrounded gaps and p."""
    for up, keep in [("recall", "precision"), ("precision", "recall")]:
        if row[f"{up}_p"] < 0.05 and row[f"{up}_gap"] > 0 and row[f"{keep}_gap"] >= 0:
            return f"promoted: {up} up, no {keep} loss"
    return "kept: no measured gain without a loss"

3g Test¶

The test guards against O2’s test failure: a final number that was tuned toward through repeated use of the same held-out cases. The test members were set aside in 2b and have not been touched. The final model is the register’s current incumbent — the last promotion, or the Niño-sign rule if nothing was promoted — fit once on all training members and scored once here; a second attempt would make this a validation set. The cell runs only when FINAL = True has been set in the Your trials cell — until then it prints test not run and touches nothing, so a Restart & Run All between trials never scores the test members. When it runs it counts its own runs and prints test run #N; #1 is the only passing value, and Entry 10 says what the counter cannot see. The cell ends with two lines that read the one score against no skill: the model’s precision and AP minus the base rate of the same test winters, each with an interval from resampling whole test members; nothing is refit to draw it. The reading is taken on the bounds as printed (3 dp): an interval whose printed bound is +0.000 or -0.000 spans zero and reads NOT distinguishable. Summary¶

The spine was walked end to end on the NSF NCAR-distributed ensemble: a question framed as a decision; a Metadata Document filled from the catalogue, not memory; the forced response removed and checked by a test it could fail; a split by ensemble member, the transforms refit inside every fold, and a contrast cell that measured the two leaks here rather than assuming them; a three-rung ladder scored against the base rate and a one-rule classifier; refinement trials compared as a paired test against the corrected spread, each p uncorrected with the Bonferroni line printed and the looks bounded at six; one test run once and read against no skill; and a decision justified on measured performance and cost, including when no model beats the simplest answer.

The design defaults, as recorded. Seven design decisions, recorded with their reasons and alternatives in the Metadata Document (2a); each is a parameter or a rule in the code, none is written into any answer: the forced response removed as a degree-FORCED_DEG fit; wet against one pooled threshold; test members drawn per half (TEST_FRACTION); N_FOLDS = min(N_FOLDS_MAX, training members); rung 0 is three rules with the Niño-sign rule as first incumbent; promotion on corrected p < 0.05 with no mean loss in the other metric; and the 3d table carries fold-mean and pooled precision/recall. ACCESS_DATE is the cache header’s access= field — written by the REBUILD = True run that made the table and asserted equal to it on every run. All of it follows scikit-learn’s rule for a rerunnable fit: declare the seed once, pass it down, and let a Pipeline keep every transform on the training side of the split.
