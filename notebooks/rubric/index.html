Handbook Rubric

Content CC BY 4.0. Shape after NSF Unidata’s “Handbook Rubric” (Supervised Machine Learning Readiness, Apache-2.0, NSF award #2319979): a self-assessment the learner applies to each handbook entry, then the trainer applies to the same entries.

How to score¶

Three levels per entry. The level is decided by the evidence pasted and what the response does with it, not by its length.

LevelMeans
Not yetevidence missing, or the response contradicts it, or a required decision is not stated
Developingevidence present; the response reads it but misses the step’s failure (the O1 table) or leaves a required part out
Proficientevidence present; every claim points at a number or line in it; the step’s failure is named and avoided; the decision is stated with its reason

Two rules that cut across every entry:

  • No evidence, no score. An entry whose evidence box is empty is Not yet regardless of the prose.

  • A gap inside the spread is not a difference. The spread is the corrected standard error the paired t divides the gap by — the register’s _t and _p columns, or the scaffold cell’s corrected standard error line; the per-fold _sd printed beside it is for scale and is not the test. Each p is per test and uncorrected (the cap of six bounds the looks; it does not correct them; the Bonferroni line printed above the register table is the corrected threshold, and a promotion whose p sits above it is a promotion under the uncorrected rule only). Any entry that reads a non-significant lead as a result (Entries 7–11) caps at Developing.

Per-objective roll-up¶

It does so the way Unidata’s series does — the learner records a response per exercise in a separate handbook, scores it against the rubric, and the trainer scores the same entries — with one addition the series does not have: a roll-up per objective, so that the handbook gives one verdict per objective. Entry 7 sits in the O1 row because it carries the validate step (and Entry 3’s evidence carries train), so the row now covers seven of the eight steps of the step → failure table — framing (1, 2), data (3, 4), split (5), train (3), validate (7), refine (8, 9) and test (10); justify is counted under O2 (Entries 9 and 11). The entries that must be Proficient are the two the step table names as the leakage and test lessons (5 and 10) and the one that carries O2’s “justify honestly” (11); the counts leave room for one weak data entry and one weak refinement entry without failing the objective. This table is the home of each entry’s objectives: the tags on the handbook’s entry headers and in the criteria table’s second column are derived from it and must match it row for row — O1 and O2 only, the two objectives this notebook serves.

ObjectiveEntriesProficient needed for “met”

O2 — build, validate, justify4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 115 of 7, including 11

Trainer notes¶

  • Score from the executed notebook the learner hands in with the handbook, not from the handbook alone: Entry 10’s “run once” criterion is read from the printed test run # line (3g runs only when the learner has set FINAL = True in the Your trials cell, so a run-all between trials scores nothing; the cell then counts its own runs within one kernel session; #1 is the only passing value; FINAL = True then Restart & Run All after the last 3f trial is the one legitimate run and leaves it at 1 — the counter cannot see a restart, so the handbook’s order, Entries 8 and 9 written before 10, with the executed register matching Entry 8 and FINAL = True in the executed Your trials cell, is the evidence that the test came last). Entry 1’s run started … line is the dated record of the run it belongs to, and the handbook’s order — Entries 1 and 2 above Entry 3’s pasted cache header — is the evidence that the entry came before 2a; the notebook cannot see when a handbook line was typed, so with the line present the entry is scored on its content. For the same reason Entry 8’s “before running” is scored on the three lines present and the row read against them.

  • A learner whose tested model shows no skill distinguishable from the base rate, and whose Entry 11 says so and recommends the base rate, has met O2. No promotion means the register’s first incumbent — the rung-0 Niño-sign rule — was the model tested in 3g; the sign rule was made incumbent as the physical prior, not because it cleared the always-wet bar, so Entry 10’s interval lines are where its skill is read. The same holds after a promotion: the 3f rule promotes over the incumbent, not over the base rate, so a promoted model’s skill is still read from the interval lines, and a learner who reads “promoted” as “skilful” caps at Developing on 10 and 11. When the signal is weak, O2’s “simplest wins” answer is the base-rate classifier. The physical prior (Stagge et al. 2023; NWS Boulder 2020) is a weak Front Range signal; a learner who reads the null as proof of that prior is attributing what the run did not test (Developing on 11).

  • The committed run’s readings, exercise by exercise, are in a trainer key, kept for instructors and available on request. The learner notebook states its method and its overall finding, never the committed run’s readings exercise by exercise.

  • Record the levels in the handbook’s self-score table, trainer column. Report the per-objective roll-up beside them.
