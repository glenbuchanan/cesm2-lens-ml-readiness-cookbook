Module 2 · Handbook Rubric

Content CC BY 4.0. Shape after NSF Unidata’s “Handbook Rubric” (Supervised Machine Learning Readiness, Apache-2.0, NSF award #2319979): a self-assessment the learner applies to each handbook entry, then the trainer applies to the same entries. Scores feed the per-objective delta that the pretest/quiz pair cannot see.

How to score¶

Three levels per entry. The level is decided by the evidence pasted and what the response does with it, not by its length.

LevelMeans
Not yetevidence missing, or the response contradicts it, or a required decision is not stated
Developingevidence present; the response reads it but misses the step’s failure (the O2 table) or leaves a required part out
Proficientevidence present; every claim points at a number or line in it; the step’s failure is named and avoided; the decision is stated with its reason

Two rules that cut across every entry:

  • No evidence, no score. An entry whose evidence box is empty is Not yet regardless of the prose.

  • A gap inside the spread is not a difference. The spread is the corrected standard error the paired t divides the gap by — the register’s _t and _p columns, or the scaffold cell’s corrected standard error line; the per-fold _sd printed beside it is for scale and is not the test. Each p is per test and uncorrected (the cap of six bounds the looks; it does not correct them; the Bonferroni line printed above the register table is the corrected threshold, and a promotion whose p sits above it is a promotion under the uncorrected rule only). Any entry that reads a non-significant lead as a result (Entries 7–11) caps at Developing.

Per-objective roll-up¶

The handbook measures the two objectives the pretest/quiz pair samples only in part. It does so the way Unidata’s series does — the learner records a response per exercise in a separate handbook, scores it against the rubric, and the trainer scores the same entries — with one addition the series does not have: a roll-up per objective, so that the handbook’s verdict can sit beside the quiz delta in the one-page report. The weights below are the skeleton’s, unchanged except one addition of 2026-10-22: Entry 7 joins the O2 row, because it carries the validate step (and Entry 3’s evidence carries train), so the row now covers seven of the eight steps of the course’s step → failure table — framing (1, 2), data (3, 4), split (5), train (3), validate (7), refine (8, 9) and test (10); justify is counted under O3 (Entries 9 and 11). The entries that must be Proficient are the two the O2 table names as the module’s leakage and test lessons (5 and 10) and the one that carries O3’s “justify honestly” (11); the counts leave room for one weak data entry and one weak refinement entry without failing the objective. This table is the home of each entry’s objectives: the tags on the handbook’s entry headers and in the criteria table’s second column are derived from it and must match it row for row — O2 and O3 only, because O1 is Module 1’s objective and the pretest/quiz’s, and no Module 2 entry is rolled up under it.

Trainer notes¶

  • Score from the executed notebook the learner hands in with the handbook, not from the handbook alone: Entry 10’s “run once” criterion is read from the printed test run # line (3g runs only when the learner has set FINAL = True in the Your trials cell, so a run-all between trials scores nothing; the cell then counts its own runs within one kernel session; #1 is the only passing value; FINAL = True then Restart & Run All after the last 3f trial is the one legitimate run and leaves it at 1 — the counter cannot see a restart, so the handbook’s order, Entries 8 and 9 written before 10, with the executed register matching Entry 8 and FINAL = True in the executed Your trials cell, is the evidence that the test came last). Entry 1’s run started … line is the dated record of the run it belongs to, and the handbook’s order — Entries 1 and 2 above Entry 3’s pasted cache header — is the evidence that the entry came before 2a; the notebook cannot see when a handbook line was typed, so with the line present the entry is scored on its content. For the same reason Entry 8’s “before running” is scored as Module 3’s note scores it: the three lines present and the row read against them.

  • A learner whose tested model shows no skill distinguishable from the base rate, and whose Entry 11 says so and recommends the base rate, has met O3. No promotion means the register’s first incumbent — the rung-0 Niño-sign rule — was the model tested in 3g; the sign rule was made incumbent as the physical prior, not because it cleared the always-wet bar, so Entry 10’s interval lines are where its skill is read. The same holds after a promotion: the 3f rule promotes over the incumbent, not over the base rate, so a promoted model’s skill is still read from the interval lines, and a learner who reads “promoted” as “skilful” caps at Developing on 10 and 11. When the signal is weak, O3’s “simplest wins” answer is the base-rate classifier. The module’s physical prior (Stagge et al. 2023; NWS Boulder 2020) is a weak Front Range signal; a learner who reads the null as proof of that prior is attributing what the run did not test (Developing on 11).

  • The committed run’s readings, exercise by exercise, are in the Module 2 trainer key, kept for instructors and available on request. The learner notebook states method, never the committed run’s results.

  • Record the levels in the handbook’s self-score table, trainer column. Report the per-objective roll-up alongside the quiz delta.

Two more rules for Module 3¶

  • A reading that crosses the line is capped. Any entry (18, 19, 22) that asserts or defends an attribution as a causal claim or a dataset-wide claim caps at Developing; the reading rule is per prediction, sign and size. A sentence written and marked as outside the line in Entry 19 is the exercise, not an assertion, and is not capped.

  • No run record, no trust verdict. Entries 20–22 are scored on the run record and the two lookups, which print without a GPU; a forecast figure is not required, but a trust verdict written without the record’s three lines — checkpoint, initial conditions, lead times — is Not yet.

Trainer notes — Module 3¶

  • Score from the executed notebook the learner hands in with the handbook. Entry 12 is scored on content (it quotes Module 2’s entries and cites no Module 3 output); Entry 17 on the printed test run # line (the cell counts its own runs within one kernel session: #1 proves there was no second run in that session and cannot see a restart — a learner who tuned against the test members, restarted and ran all prints #1 — so the handbook’s order, Entries 15 and 16 written before 17, is the evidence that the test was run once; #2 or higher is a second test, Not yet); Entry 16’s “stated before it ran” on the learner’s three lines being present and the row read against them; Entry 13’s identical test members on the two notebooks’ printed lines.

  • A Module 2 model tested in 3g has no epoch line. When the register promotes nothing — the outcome Exercise 12 (c) asks the learner to anticipate — the final model is the learner’s INCUMBENT_FROM_MODULE_2 (a logistic, a tree or boosting), and 3g prints no validation minimum at epoch line; Entry 17 is then scored on the block, the rung-0 bars and the two interval lines, and no epoch line is asked for. The Not-yet cell’s “a network tested with no epoch line” applies only when a network was tested.

  • The third trial is the learner’s. Two trials ship; record_trial refuses a fourth tested row (the no-skill control is a check, not a trial). A learner who runs a trial without the three lines, or who writes the lines to match the row afterward, caps at Developing on 16.

  • The 3f rule promotes over the incumbent and over the base rate, not over a physical prior. A promotion means a decision number rose measurably with AP measurably above both the incumbent’s and the base rate; the tested model’s skill is still read from the 3g interval lines, and a learner who reads “promoted” as “skilful” caps at Developing on 17 and 22.

  • RUN_INFERENCE = False is the expected state on a laptop; the contrast case is printed by 5b, 5d and 5e and is never run. Entries 20–22 are complete without a forecast figure; when the GPU cell was run, the figure must carry the three-notice credit the notebook prints (Microsoft MIT · ECMWF initial conditions · Copernicus static fields). The course ran the cell once, on 2026-10-22, on one NVIDIA L40S 48 GB (g6e.4xlarge): ran 2 steps from 2022-05-11; output grid 720×1440, 92.8 s at a peak of 40,325 MiB; the run’s record is kept by the author and available on request; 5c shows a re-plot of that output. A learner’s own ran N steps line is recorded by the trainer.

  • Entry 21’s lead-time axis and state lines must be the printed ones, with the version line (read date, Last-Modified, ETag) above each block — two learners a year apart may paste different numbers, and that line says why. If either store could not be read in the learner’s session, the printed could not read line carries no numbers and the entry is Developing until the cell is re-run online. A learner who calls the default case “trusted because 2022 is a test year” without the state lines is Developing; one who reads an in-sample date as condition (1), or as “trusted because it is inside the training range”, is Not yet (the leakage lesson read backwards — the record labels IN-SAMPLE as no evidence of skill, and OUTSIDE as the band where condition (1) fires); one who reads the contrast case’s lead times without its start-hour line is Developing.
