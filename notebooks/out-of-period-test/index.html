An out-of-period test, under a protocol fixed before the run

Chapter 6’s held-out test cannot settle whether the final model has skill: the model was chosen after its validation score was seen, and the test members had been scored during development. This chapter runs the test that is left — the same members’ future winters under SSP3-7.0, a period no choice in this cookbook was made on — under a protocol fixed before the scored run (self-attested; no external timestamp).

Overview¶

  1. Why chapter 6’s test is not blind, and what a fresh test needs.

  2. The protocol, fixed before the scored run, with its decision rule.

  3. The future-period transforms, fitted on the 80 training members’ future winters only.

  4. The frozen models, fitted exactly as chapter 6 fits them.

  5. The single scored run: the primary outcome, then the secondary outcomes the protocol lists.

  6. The reading against the rule, and the limits.

Shared code lives in lensml.py beside the chapters (parameters, transforms, ladder, scoring, paired test, register); show() prints a function’s source where a chapter introduces it.

Why chapter 6’s test is not blind¶

Two things were done with the 20 test members before chapter 6 shows them. During development (2026-10-20) they were scored with two candidate final models: logistic at the base-rate threshold, which showed no detectable edge, and boosting at the base-rate threshold, the result chapter 6 prints. And the final model itself was chosen after chapter 3 had already scored it on the training folds. A fresh test needs data no choice was made on. Every ensemble member has been used — 80 drove every choice, 20 have been scored — so what is left untouched is the same members’ future winters: the ssp370 stores of the same ensemble, 2016–2100.

The protocol, fixed before the scored run¶

The protocol was written on 2026-10-29, before any SSP3-7.0 data were read, and is followed here without change. That order is self-attested: there is no external timestamp. The protocol ships verbatim as data/ssp370-test-protocol.md; the cell below prints its SHA-256, so a reader can check the shipped file is the one quoted. Its decision rule, in its own words:

Primary outcome (decided now): precision − base rate for boosting@base-rate on the test members’ future winters, with the same resampling-over-test-members 95% interval as chapter 6. “Edge replicates” if the interval’s lower bound is above 0; “does not replicate” otherwise. Secondary, reported whatever they show: AP − base rate with interval; recall and wet-call share; false alarms per hit; the same numbers for the comparators; the decile wet rates on the training edges with a chi-square test.

Frozen with it: the model is boosting@base-rate exactly as chapter 6 fits it (all 80 historical training members, the same seed, hyperparameters and threshold); the comparators are always-wet, always-dry, the Niño-sign rule and logistic@base-rate, fitted the same way; nothing is refitted on future data. The forced response and the wet threshold for the future period are fitted on the 80 training members’ future winters only, then applied to the test members. One clarification was made before the run, where the protocol left it open: “the training edges” are chapter 2’s decile edges of the training members, the bins chapter 6 used. The scoring code, score_frozen, was checked before the run against chapter 6: on the historical test members it reproduces chapter 6’s interval lines exactly.

Future-period transforms, from the training members only¶

Under SSP3-7.0 the forced path is steeper than in the historical period, so the historical curve cannot be reused: a degree-FORCED_DEG curve and the pooled WET_QUANTILE threshold are refitted, on the training members’ future winters only. The decade check of chapter 2 is repeated on those training members; it reads no test row.

The frozen models¶

Each model is fitted once, on the 80 training members’ historical winters, exactly as chapter 6 fits its final model; the final model’s factory is the register’s trial 5. Nothing below refits.

X_tr, y_tr = S.train[FEATURES].values, S.train['wet'].values
name5, make5, _ = TRIALS[4]
assert name5 == 'boosting@base-rate'
models = {
    'boosting@base-rate': make5(RNG_SEED).fit(X_tr, y_tr),
    'always-wet': ladder(RNG_SEED)['always-wet'].fit(X_tr, y_tr),
    'always-dry': ladder(RNG_SEED)['always-dry'].fit(X_tr, y_tr),
    'nino-sign': ladder(RNG_SEED)['nino-sign'].fit(X_tr, y_tr),
    'logistic@base-rate': at_base_rate('logistic')(RNG_SEED).fit(X_tr, y_tr),
}
edges = decile_edges(S.train)  # chapter 2's training edges
print('frozen:', ', '.join(models))

The scored run¶

This cell is the test. It prints the primary outcome first, then the secondary outcomes in the protocol’s order: for each model, precision and AP minus the base rate with their intervals, then recall, wet-call share and false alarms per hit; then the decile table with its chi-square test. A counter marks any re-run in the same kernel; a rebuild recomputes the same numbers and changes nothing.

SCORED_RUNS = globals().get('SCORED_RUNS', 0) + 1
print(
    f'scored run #{SCORED_RUNS}'
    + ('' if SCORED_RUNS == 1 else '   — a re-run in this kernel')
)
fut_test = fut[fut['split'].eq('test')].reset_index(drop=True)
buf = io.StringIO()
with contextlib.redirect_stdout(buf):
    results = score_frozen(models, fut_test, edges)
pt, lo, hi = results['boosting@base-rate']['precision − base rate']
verdict = 'EDGE REPLICATES' if lo > 0 else 'DOES NOT REPLICATE'

Reading the run against the rule¶

The edge does not replicate under the rule fixed before the run. The PRIMARY OUTCOME line is the result: on the test members’ future winters, boosting at the base-rate threshold has a precision whose 95% interval over test members includes zero. The line after the secondary outcomes shows the interval also includes chapter 6’s estimate, so this test cannot separate no edge from an edge of that size. It gives no support for leaning an outlook wet.

The secondary outcomes say the same from other sides. AP, which reads the ranking at every threshold, is not distinguishable from no skill for the final model either (its AP − base rate line above). No comparator clears the base rate on precision (the comparator lines printed under the primary outcome): the Niño-sign rule and logistic at the base-rate threshold sit inside the no-skill band, and always-wet sits on it by construction; always-dry calls nothing wet, so its precision is undefined (scored 0). Every model that calls wet makes more false alarms than hits (the false alarms per hit values), as precision below 0.5 must. The decile table’s chi-square line says whether the wet rate differs between the training bins on these winters; read it beside chapter 2’s and chapter 6’s — the coldest bin is the driest here, as in chapter 2, while in chapter 6’s test members the warmest was slightly drier; the omnibus test does not reject chance.

What this does not say: it does not show the historical edge was an artefact — a shift in period and forcing could hide a real but fragile relation (limit 1). It does say that the chapter 6 edge, chosen after its validation score was seen and scored on members already used, is not evidence a forecaster could lean on.

Limits¶

  1. A different period under stronger forcing. SSP3-7.0 winters are a shifted distribution: the model was fit on 1900–2014 and scored on 2016–2100, with its own forced response refitted, and the input itself is wider: the SON Niño 3.4 anomaly SD line compares the training members’ spread in the two periods. A failure can be shift as well as no skill; a success is skill that survived it.

  2. A model world. CESM2 future winters scored against CESM2 future winters; nothing here touches observations.

  3. The same members. The 20 test members are the ones scored during development; their future winters are new data, but not new realisations of the model’s internal variability from scratch.

  4. The existing limits. Chapter 6’s limits 2–7 hold unchanged: CESM2’s own ENSO and teleconnection, a ~1° grid, DJF rather than late winter–spring, total precipitation, one forced curve for both halves, a box that crosses the Divide.

Summary¶

A protocol fixed the model, comparators, outcomes and decision rule before any SSP3-7.0 data were read. The forced response and the wet threshold for the future period were fitted on the training members’ future winters only, and the frozen models were scored once on the test members’ future winters. The primary outcome does not replicate under the rule fixed before the run: the final model’s precision interval includes zero, and also chapter 6’s estimate, so the test cannot separate no edge from an edge of that size. It gives no support for leaning an outlook wet.

What’s next?¶

The same protocol could be run in another season (FMA), another box or another ensemble, each written down before its data are read. Observed winters remain the test that would tell a model result from a real one.
