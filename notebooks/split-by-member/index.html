Splitting by ensemble member

Unidata’s notebooks split “randomly”. Here the unit of the split is the ensemble member, and the cost of getting it wrong is measured rather than assumed.

Overview¶

  1. Build member-grouped validation folds with scikit-learn; verify no member sits on both sides of a fold.

  2. Measure the dependence that would make a member-blind split leak: within members (lag-1) and between micro-siblings.

  3. Score one model three ways — this cookbook’s pipeline, transforms fit before the split, a member-blind split — and read the gaps.

Loading and the transforms come from lensml.py; the splitter and scoring loop are written out here.

Why the member is the unit¶

scikit-learn’s guidance on grouped cross-validation: “all the samples in the validation fold come from groups that are not represented at all in the paired training fold.” Three reasons make the member the group:

  1. The question is about members never seen. A member-blind split scores new years of members the model already knows.

  2. The transforms. Fit before the split, held-out winters would help fit the forced curve and wet threshold that label them — the leak of a StandardScaler fit on the whole table. Both are refit inside each fold.

  3. Dependence is measured, not assumed. After the fold check come the lag-1 table and a micro-sibling line that fails if members sharing a start year move together; that line licenses not grouping siblings.

Micro-siblings, and why one window is not enough

Rodgers et al. 2021 §2.2 warn of initial-condition memory after 1850 and analyse internal variability “after 1960”; they give no figure for 1900, so this cookbook’s 1900 start rests on the check below, not on Rodgers. Micro-siblings are members sharing a start year, as the CESM2 LE project page defines them — the i1231-style token in member_id. The check correlates them against unrelated members over the window.

Beside chapter 2’s test members, the training members are split into N_FOLDS = min(N_FOLDS_MAX, training members) folds by scikit-learn’s StratifiedGroupKFold: the groups keep each member whole, and stratifying balances wet winters across folds.

Which labels the stratification reads

The folds are stratified on the all-training labels, which decide only which members share a fold; the labels scored inside a fold are refit on that fold’s training members.

The leak contrast: one model, three ways¶

One loop scores every validation run in this cookbook. Per fold: refit the transforms on the fold’s training rows (unless refit_transforms=False), .fit the model, .predict the held-out rows, and score them for the wet class.

def score_folds(model, rows, splits, refit_transforms=True):
    out = {m: [] for m in ['ap', 'precision', 'recall', 'accuracy']}
    cm = np.zeros((2, 2), dtype=int)  # rows = true, columns = predicted
    for a, b in splits:
        fit_rows, held_rows = rows.iloc[a], rows.iloc[b]
        if refit_transforms:  # curve and threshold from this fold only
            tf = fit_transforms(fit_rows)
            fit_rows = apply_transforms(fit_rows, tf)
            held_rows = apply_transforms(held_rows, tf)
        Xa, ya = fit_rows[FEATURES].values, fit_rows['wet'].values
        Xb, yb = held_rows[FEATURES].values, held_rows['wet'].values
        model.fit(Xa, ya)
        yhat = model.predict(Xb)
        p_wet = model.predict_proba(Xb)[:, 1]
        out['precision'].append(precision_score(yb, yhat, zero_division=0))
        out['recall'].append(recall_score(yb, yhat, zero_division=0))
        out['accuracy'].append(accuracy_score(yb, yhat))
        out['ap'].append(average_precision_score(yb, p_wet))
        cm += confusion_matrix(yb, yhat, labels=[0, 1])
    return {m: np.array(v) for m, v in out.items()}, cm

How to read the gaps, and what this cell costs chapter 5

A precision resting on a handful of calls makes any gap a non-test, so below one wet call per hundred winters the cell marks the gaps NON-TEST. The ± beside each gap is the SD of the per-fold gaps — a rough scale; chapter 5’s paired test is the measurement.

This cell is a validation score for boosting at the base-rate threshold, on the same seeds chapter 5 uses. Once it has been read, a trial of that model is no longer a blind one.

Reading this cell un-blinds chapter 5’s trial of this model (see the note).

Why the gaps are small here¶

Read each arm’s gap vs A line against the ± beside it: the leaks barely bite. The mean: line under the lag-1 table gives lag1_wet near zero, so arm C has little to learn from a member’s other years. A small gap measures this design; it is not a licence.

Why the gaps are small, and when they would not be

  • No within-member persistence to memorise. With lag1_wet near zero, one wet winter says almost nothing about the next in the same member. The input’s lag-1 is negative, not positive — no run of similar autumns either.

  • The input carries no member identity. With one detrended Niño 3.4 value per winter, nothing in a row tells the model which member it came from, so it cannot memorise a member even when it has seen one.

  • The transforms are smooth fits to many members. A cubic in year through the mean of many training members, and one pooled quantile, barely move when one fold’s members are left out — so fitting them before the split (arm B) leaks almost nothing.

With a member-specific input (a member’s own past winters, say) or transforms fit on fewer members, the same cell is where a member split earns its keep.

Try it: the tidier pipeline

A colleague fits the forced curve and the wet threshold on the whole table, test members included, before any split. Say what the held-out test in chapter 6 would then measure. Then read arm B’s line: is its gap distinguishable from the per-fold SD beside it?

Summary¶

Validation folds are grouped by member with StratifiedGroupKFold and verified leak-free; the sibling check licenses the member as the unit. Scoring one model three ways turned the two leaks into numbers — small on this design.
