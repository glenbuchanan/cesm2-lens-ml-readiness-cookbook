# Handbook Rubric

*Content CC BY 4.0. Shape after NSF Unidata's "Handbook Rubric" (Supervised Machine Learning Readiness, Apache-2.0, NSF award #2319979): a self-assessment the learner applies to each handbook entry, then the trainer applies to the same entries.* 

## How to score

Three levels per entry. The level is decided by the **evidence** pasted and what the response does with it, not by its length.

| Level | Means |
|---|---|
| **Not yet** | evidence missing, or the response contradicts it, or a required decision is not stated |
| **Developing** | evidence present; the response reads it but misses the step's failure (the O1 table) or leaves a required part out |
| **Proficient** | evidence present; every claim points at a number or line in it; the step's failure is named and avoided; the decision is stated with its reason |

Two rules that cut across every entry:
- **No evidence, no score.** An entry whose evidence box is empty is *Not yet* regardless of the prose.
- **A gap inside the spread is not a difference.** The spread is the corrected standard error the paired t divides the gap by — the register's `_t` and `_p` columns, or the scaffold cell's `corrected standard error` line; the per-fold `_sd` printed beside it is for scale and is not the test. Each `p` is per test and uncorrected (the cap of six bounds the looks; it does not correct them; the Bonferroni line printed above the register table is the corrected threshold, and a promotion whose `p` sits above it is a promotion under the uncorrected rule only). Any entry that reads a non-significant lead as a result (Entries 7–11) caps at *Developing*. 

## The criteria

| Entry | Objective · O1 step | Not yet | Developing | Proficient |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · expected sign | O1 *framing* | no sign stated, (c) names no `SEASON`, or the `run started …` line not pasted | sign stated with the NWS source; (b) or (c) missing, or (c) gives the choice without its cost | the `run started …` line pasted; sign, the season's effect on the signal, and the `SEASON` choice with its reason and its cost (DJF shipped; FMA a rebuild: roughly 160 GB, 33 minutes on the one full derivation, an hour or more on home broadband) |
| 2 · three framing answers | O1 *framing* | a question unanswered, or the target is not a decision | all three answered; the three simple solutions not all named, or the closed-form distinction missing | the three simple solutions named — always-dry as the accuracy bar, always-wet as the precision bar at the base rate, the sign rule as the physical prior, no bar (the same three Entry 6 scores); closed-form case distinguished; the question written as a decision in the learner's own line, with the person who acts on it, and where it differs from the notebook's noted |
| 3 · Metadata Document | O1 *data*; O1 *train* | table not copied, or a cell copied that the printed evidence contradicts | table copied; a *Quality* row has no bias sentence, the window reason is missing, or no cell is marked *checked* or *not printed this run* | every cell the printed output can show is marked *checked* against it (corrected where it disagreed) and the cells it cannot show are marked *not printed this run*; every Quality row carries a bias sentence; window and detrending decisions recorded with reasons; the seed, version lines and cache header pasted (the record that makes the fit rerunnable) |
| 4 · the two halves | O1 *data*; O2 | no decision, or a sign read that the pasted correlation lines contradict | decision stated; the per-half tables not both pasted, reason does not use them, or the Entry 1 sign not checked against the correlation lines | the decision (pool, or not — with keeping one half named as the costed alternative for Entry 11: a filter on the table, half the members and the power) follows from the pasted base rates and correlations, reason stated; says whether the sign expected in Entry 1 appeared, read from the pasted `per-member sign count` and `per-half mean correlation` lines |
| 5 · the leak you cannot see in a split | O1 *split* | assertion lines or contrast lines missing, or the fit-everything-before-the-split pipeline defended, or an inflation reported that the pasted lines do not print | names the leak (curve and threshold fit on test members) but does not read the contrast, reads a gap in the direction assumed rather than the one measured, does not read it against its per-fold SD, or reads a precision/recall gap the block marks NON-TEST as a result | (a) names the fit-on-all-members leak and the spine step the fit belongs to; (b1) reads arms B and C against A with sign and size, however small, where the arm's wet-call count makes the gap readable, and names a precision/recall gap a non-test where the block prints NON-TEST (wet calls under one in a hundred winters), reading AP alone there; (b2) reads each gap against the per-fold SD printed beside it (scale, not a test); (b3) says what each arm measures (A new members; B new members scored with transforms they helped fit; C new years of known members); (c1) says what a member-blind split could memorise when the one input carries no member identity (little); (c2) reads the lag-1 line for what a member's winters share; (c3) reads the sibling line as what licenses member, not start-year, as the unit; pastes both assertion lines, the lag-1 line, the sibling lines and the contrast block (its header and the two lines per arm) |
| 6 · the bar | O2 | a rung-0 row missing | rows pasted; base rate not recorded alongside, or no row named as a bar on a metric | all three rung-0 rows with accuracy, AP and the base rate; says which base-rate row bars which metric (always-wet = the precision bar at the base rate; always-dry = the accuracy bar) and records the sign rule as `physical prior — no bar`, with its precision placed above or below the always-wet bar; one sentence on what they mean for the ladder |
| 7 · read the matrices | O2 *validate*; O1 *validate* | hand calculation absent or wrong by convention (rows/columns swapped) | calculation right; the beats-nino-sign question, the always-wet-precision question or the recall-1.0 question unanswered, `precision_fold` vs pooled not explained, or the four counts not relabelled in MET's vocabulary | calculation right for `nino-sign` and checked against `precision_pooled` / `recall_pooled`, with why `precision_fold` can differ; the four counts relabelled as hits / false alarms / misses / correct rejections with POD (= recall) and FAR (= 1 − precision) named and frequency bias reported where precision and recall disagree; on the pooled columns (`precision_pooled` / `recall_pooled`), the fitted rung (1–3) that beats nino-sign on both numbers named (or "none" — rung 0's always-wet beats it on recall by construction, and on precision whenever the sign rule's precision sits below the base rate, Entry 6 recording which side the learner's is on; it is read as the bar, not as an answer) and the rung whose `precision_pooled` clears the always-wet bar named (or "none"), with a note on whether `precision_fold` agrees for that rung and, if not, why; always-wet's recall 1.0 at base-rate precision read as "predicts wet always" — unbeatable on recall and therefore a bar on precision, not a model |
| 8 · trial register | O1 *refine*; O2 | register missing, more than six tested rows in all (the three rungs and the example trial count; two are the learner's), no trial of the learner's own, or a trial's three lines absent | the three lines present and the register pasted; a row not read against its three lines, a row's reading does not say which metric moved outside the spread, or ignores the other metric | the learner's own trial(s) with the three lines present (one change, a reason, an expected metric) and each row read against them; every row read against its `recall_p` / `precision_p` and both gaps, a promoted row against the Bonferroni line; considered-but-not-run trials listed with reasons; promotion only on a measured gain in one metric with no mean loss in the other (the 3f rule) |

| 10 · the one test | O1 *test*; O2 | test block missing, a `test not run` message pasted in its place (`FINAL` never set to `True`), or its `test run #` line missing or reading 2 or higher (the cell counts its own runs; `FINAL = True` then Restart & Run All after the last 3f trial is the one legitimate run and leaves it at 1) | block pasted with `test run #1`; rung-0 lines or the two interval lines missing, the tested model's skill not read against no skill, or no reason given for a validation–test gap | block from its `test run #1` line, rung-0 lines and the two interval lines pasted; skill read against no skill from the intervals (a gap whose interval spans zero is not skill — read on the bounds as the line prints them, at 3 dp: an interval whose printed lower bound is `+0.000` or lower and whose printed upper bound is `-0.000` or higher spans zero, and the line reads `NOT distinguishable from no skill`; `above` needs a printed lower bound of `+0.001` or more; `below` needs a printed upper bound of `-0.001` or lower — also not skill); validation vs test compared; one honest reason for a gap (few test members; a different base rate; a lucky fold draw) |
| 11 · final decision | O2 *justify* | a part missing, a claim with no quoted line behind it from Entries 6–10, or skill claimed for a model whose interval lines span zero | all three parts; a no-skill result hedged or omitted; skill claimed on one of the two interval lines when the other reads `NOT distinguishable` (the one-of-two rule); or the null attributed to the physics (the transition zone, the season) as a finding, when the run did not test it | every claim quotes the line it rests on (the interval lines, the register's incumbent line and verdict, the rung-0 rows); says plainly whether any tested model showed skill distinguishable from the base rate — the one-of-two rule: skill is claimed only when BOTH of Entry 10's interval lines (precision − base rate, AP − base rate) read `above no skill`; one line `above` and the other `NOT distinguishable` is not skill, the recommendation is then the base rate, "equal chances", and the entry says so — and, when none did, recommends the base rate ("equal chances"); keeps what the run measured apart from what it cannot attribute and names what would tell the explanations apart; the next change costed |

## Per-objective roll-up

 It does so the way Unidata's series does — the learner records a response per exercise in a separate handbook, scores it against the rubric, and the trainer scores the same entries — with one addition the series does not have: a roll-up per objective, so that the handbook gives one verdict per objective. Entry 7 sits in the O1 row because it carries the *validate* step (and Entry 3's evidence carries *train*), so the row now covers seven of the eight steps of the step → failure table — framing (1, 2), data (3, 4), split (5), train (3), validate (7), refine (8, 9) and test (10); justify is counted under O2 (Entries 9 and 11). The entries that must be Proficient are the two the step table names as the leakage and test lessons (5 and 10) and the one that carries O2's "justify honestly" (11); the counts leave room for one weak data entry and one weak refinement entry without failing the objective. This table is the home of each entry's objectives: the tags on the handbook's entry headers and in the criteria table's second column are derived from it and must match it row for row — O1 and O2 only, the two objectives this notebook serves. 

| Objective | Entries | Proficient needed for "met" |
|---|---|---|
| O1 — the spine and its failures | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10 | 7 of 9, including 5 (split) and 10 (test) |
| O2 — build, validate, justify | 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 | 5 of 7, including 11 |

## Trainer notes

- Score from the **executed** notebook the learner hands in with the handbook, not from the handbook alone: Entry 10's "run once" criterion is read from the printed `test run #` line (3g runs only when the learner has set `FINAL = True` in the *Your trials* cell, so a run-all between trials scores nothing; the cell then counts its own runs within one kernel session; `#1` is the only passing value; `FINAL = True` then Restart & Run All after the last 3f trial is the one legitimate run and leaves it at 1 — the counter cannot see a restart, so the handbook's order, Entries 8 and 9 written before 10, with the executed register matching Entry 8 and `FINAL = True` in the executed *Your trials* cell, is the evidence that the test came last). Entry 1's `run started …` line is the dated record of the run it belongs to, and the handbook's order — Entries 1 and 2 above Entry 3's pasted cache header — is the evidence that the entry came before 2a; the notebook cannot see when a handbook line was typed, so with the line present the entry is scored on its content. For the same reason Entry 8's "before running" is scored on the three lines present and the row read against them.
- A learner whose tested model shows **no skill distinguishable from the base rate**, and whose Entry 11 says so and recommends the base rate, has met O2. No promotion means the register's first incumbent — the rung-0 Niño-sign rule — was the model tested in 3g; the sign rule was made incumbent as the physical prior, not because it cleared the always-wet bar, so Entry 10's interval lines are where its skill is read. The same holds after a promotion: the 3f rule promotes over the incumbent, not over the base rate, so a promoted model's skill is still read from the interval lines, and a learner who reads "promoted" as "skilful" caps at *Developing* on 10 and 11. When the signal is weak, O2's "simplest wins" answer is the base-rate classifier. The physical prior (Stagge et al. 2023; NWS Boulder 2020) is a weak Front Range signal; a learner who reads the null as *proof* of that prior is attributing what the run did not test (*Developing* on 11).
- The committed run's readings, exercise by exercise, are in a trainer key, kept for instructors and available on request. The learner notebook states its method and its overall finding, never the committed run's readings exercise by exercise.
- Record the levels in the handbook's self-score table, trainer column. Report the per-objective roll-up beside them.
